My provisional call before critique was **watch/defer**. The easiest fact that could change my mind is evidence that ACI's normalized earnings and cash generation can recover while debt declines; the next annual reported operating-cash-flow-less-capex and the effect of opioid payments are especially important.

**Skeptical AI-colleague criticism:** The weakest supported assumption is treating ACI's $0.40 FY2025 reported diluted EPS as a recurring earnings base even though the 10-K reports an opioid-settlement-framework charge. That makes the P/E range mechanically low relative to a normalized view, while the DCF begins from FCFF rather than EPS. The valuation objects are both per-share equity values and the date matches, but the earnings/cash-flow bases do not directly match. **Question:** What primary-source evidence shows that the opioid charge and related cash payments will not materially reduce future cash flow or recurring earnings?

**My judgment of the criticism: accept.** The source supports the existence of the charge, and I have not sourced a normalized GAAP EPS or a schedule proving the cash-flow effect is immaterial. I do **not** replace the lab's reported-EPS input with adjusted EPS, because that would change the stated basis. Instead, I treat the P/E result as a conservative, charge-sensitive comparison and withhold a precise P/E fair-value point estimate. The next annual filing's settlement-payment disclosure and reported earnings/cash flow would resolve this most directly.
