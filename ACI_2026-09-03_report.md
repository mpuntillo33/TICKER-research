# Albertsons Companies (ACI) — 2nd Draft Analysis Summary

**As of:** September 3, 2026  
**Scope:** FY2025 10-K (year ended February 28, 2026), updated for the latest filed 10-Q (16 weeks ended June 20, 2026). This is research for coursework, not individualized investment advice.

## Bottom line

ACI is a large, cash-generative grocery operator, but the near-term evidence is deteriorating: first-quarter identical sales, adjusted EBITDA, margin, and adjusted EPS all declined, while interest expense and debt remain material. [Q1 FY2026 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1646972/000164697226000046/aci-20260620.htm) The draft conclusion is **Watch / Defer** because there is not yet evidence that the earnings and margin trend has stabilized.

## Business in one minute

Albertsons operates 2,244 food-and-drug stores across 35 states and D.C. under 22 local banners, including Albertsons, Safeway, Vons, Jewel-Osco, ACME, Shaw’s, Tom Thumb, and United Supermarkets. It also operates pharmacies, fuel centers, delivery/pickup, private-label products, and a retail-media business. [FY2025 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1646972/000164697226000032/aci-20260228.htm)

The attractive features are scale, local store brands, pharmacy, loyalty/data, digital fulfillment, and $16.9bn of FY2025 Own Brands retail sales. [FY2025 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1646972/000164697226000032/aci-20260228.htm) The tradeoff is that grocery is a low-margin, highly competitive business, so modest cost or sales pressure can produce a large change in equity earnings.

## Original-draft bridge — retained and updated

Your original judgment remains sound: **net debt is a mechanical enterprise-value-to-equity-value bridge, not a conclusion about debt sustainability or fair value.** It also correctly notes that debt, lease, and EBITDA definitions must be treated consistently.

The latest reported balance-sheet inputs are $9.163bn of debt and finance-lease obligations ($746.1m current plus $8.417bn long term) and $293.4m cash, implying **$8.869bn net debt**. This calculation includes finance leases and assumes reported cash offsets debt; $136.6m of cash was segregated for the first opioid settlement payment, so usable cash may be lower for valuation purposes. [Q1 FY2026 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1646972/000164697226000046/aci-20260620.htm)

```text
Net debt = $9,162.8m debt and finance leases − $293.4m cash
         = $8,869.4m

Equity value = enterprise value − net debt
```

This report deliberately does not use a share-price, market-capitalization, or trading-multiple conclusion because a dated, permanently citable valuation-date quote has not yet been added. The net-debt bridge is retained for use once that source is available. It must not be treated as intrinsic value, a normalized earnings estimate, or an adjustment for opioid obligations.

## What has changed since the 10-K

The first reported quarter of FY2026 was weaker than the annual baseline. Revenue grew 0.2% to $24.94bn, primarily from fuel, while identical sales excluding fuel declined 0.8%. Digital sales still grew 13%, but that did not offset the core-sales decline. [Q1 FY2026 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1646972/000164697226000046/aci-20260620.htm)

Margins also moved the wrong way: gross margin declined to 26.6% from 27.1%, adjusted EBITDA fell to $1.013bn from $1.111bn, and adjusted EBITDA margin fell to 4.1% from 4.5%. GAAP net income declined to $84.7m from $236.4m; adjusted net income fell to $210.3m from $318.9m, or $0.42 from $0.55 per adjusted diluted share. [Q1 FY2026 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1646972/000164697226000046/aci-20260620.htm)

Liquidity is not the immediate issue: at June 20, ACI reported $293m cash and $3.29bn of ABL availability. But the balance sheet is important because first-quarter interest expense rose to $166.7m from $141.8m, debt/finance-lease obligations were $9.16bn, and the opioid settlement framework involves payments over nine years. [Q1 FY2026 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1646972/000164697226000046/aci-20260620.htm)

Management is attempting an operating reset. In July it announced **ACI Edge**, consolidating 11 divisions into four regions and centralizing center-store merchandising. This may improve accountability and scale, but benefits are prospective rather than proven. [Q1 FY2026 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1646972/000164697226000046/aci-20260620.htm)

## Investment call — Watch / Defer

This is a draft judgment for review, not a claim about the reader’s personal view. ACI merits continued monitoring because its large banner network, digital growth, loyalty base, Own Brands, and operating cash generation provide potential sources of resilience and improvement. [FY2025 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1646972/000164697226000032/aci-20260228.htm) However, initiating a position now is premature: Q1 comparable sales declined 0.8%, adjusted EBITDA margin fell 40 bps to 4.1%, adjusted EPS declined from $0.55 to $0.42, and interest expense increased. [Q1 FY2026 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1646972/000164697226000046/aci-20260620.htm) The debt load, planned opioid payments, capital-expenditure needs, and unproven ACI Edge reorganization reinforce the need for confirmation before acting. [Q1 FY2026 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1646972/000164697226000046/aci-20260620.htm)

**What would change this call:** Move to **initiate buy** if the next reported quarter shows positive comparable-sales growth and stable-or-improving adjusted EBITDA margin, with net debt declining or clearly supported by cash flow after capex, opioid payments, and shareholder distributions.
